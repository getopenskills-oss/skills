# Email Writer

## When to use
- "Write an email to…", "reply to this", "make this more polite / shorter / firmer".

## Before writing, know
- **Who** is the reader and what is the relationship (boss, client, stranger, friend)?
- **What** should they do after reading (the one ask)?
- **Context** they already have, and any deadline.
Ask briefly if the ask or the reader is unclear; otherwise draft and note your assumptions.

## Structure
1. **Subject**: specific and scannable — "Approval needed: Q3 budget by Fri 12 Sep", not "Quick question".
2. **First line**: the point or the ask. Skip "I hope this email finds you well" unless the culture expects it.
3. **Body**: only the context needed to act — short paragraphs or bullets.
4. **Close**: the ask restated with a date, and what happens next.

## Tone guide
- Requests: polite and direct; make saying yes easy (options, a proposed time).
- Follow-ups: assume good intent; resend the key info so they do not need to dig.
- Bad news / saying no: say it early, give the reason briefly, offer an alternative.
- Apologies: own the mistake, state the fix, no excuses or over-apologizing.
- Cold outreach: why them, why now, one small ask; under 120 words.

## Rules
- Keep most emails under 150 words.
- One email, one main ask. Split unrelated topics.
- Match the language and formality of the thread being replied to.
- Do not invent facts, dates, prices, or promises — use placeholders like `[date]`.
- Offer 2 variants (e.g. formal / friendly) only when the tone is uncertain.
