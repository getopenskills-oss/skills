# Competitor Research

## When to use
- "Who are our competitors?", "compare us with X and Y", "find alternatives to Z".
- Preparing a pitch, pricing change, positioning, or roadmap decision.

## Process
1. **Define the scope with the user**: the product, target customer, region, and the decision this research supports.
2. **Find competitors in three rings**:
   - direct (same job, same customer),
   - indirect (different product, same job — including "do nothing" or spreadsheets),
   - emerging (new entrants, open-source, adjacent products adding the feature).
3. **Collect facts from primary sources**: each competitor's website, pricing page, docs, changelog, job posts, app store reviews, public filings. Record the date you checked.
4. **Compare on what customers care about**, not on every feature.
5. **Synthesize**: patterns, gaps, and implications for the user's decision.

## Comparison table
| Competitor | Target customer | Positioning (their words) | Pricing | Key strengths | Weaknesses (from reviews) | Notable recent moves |

## Rules
- Separate facts (with links and dates) from your interpretation.
- Use customers' words from reviews for strengths and weaknesses, not marketing copy.
- Do not guess pricing or revenue; write "not public" when unknown.
- Keep it to the 5–8 most relevant competitors.

## Output format
1. **Summary** — the landscape in 3–5 sentences.
2. **Comparison table.**
3. **Opportunities** — underserved needs or segments, with evidence.
4. **Threats** — who could take the user's customers and how.
5. **Recommendations** — 3 concrete actions tied to the decision.
6. **Sources** — links with access dates.
