# Pitch Deck Writer

## When to use
- "Help me with my pitch deck", "make an investor deck", "turn this into a 10-slide presentation".

## Before writing, gather
- What the company does, for whom, and why now.
- Real traction (users, revenue, growth, pilots, waitlist) with dates.
- The audience (pre-seed angel, VC, customer, competition jury) and the ask (amount, use of funds).
Ask for missing facts; never fill gaps with invented numbers.

## Standard structure (10–12 slides)
1. **Title** — name, one-line description, contact.
2. **Problem** — who has it, how painful, how they cope today (a concrete story or number).
3. **Solution** — what you do and the key insight; a product screenshot beats a paragraph.
4. **Why now** — the change (technology, regulation, behavior) that makes this possible now.
5. **Product** — how it works, 2–3 core features tied to the problem.
6. **Market** — bottom-up sizing (customers × price) rather than a top-down "1% of a huge market".
7. **Traction** — the strongest real metric, as a chart over time.
8. **Business model** — how you make money, pricing, unit economics if known.
9. **Competition** — honest positioning; what you do that others cannot easily copy.
10. **Go-to-market** — how you reach the first 100 / 1,000 customers.
11. **Team** — why this team wins; relevant experience only.
12. **The ask** — amount, milestones it reaches, use of funds.

## Writing rules
- One idea per slide; the slide title states the takeaway ("Revenue grew 3× in 6 months").
- Under ~30 words of body text per slide; put detail in speaker notes.
- Use the customer's language, not internal jargon.
- Every number has a source or is clearly labeled as an estimate.

## Output
For each slide: title (the takeaway), on-slide content (bullets or a visual suggestion), and 2–4 sentences of speaker notes. End with the 3 questions investors are most likely to ask and suggested answers.
