# Job Description Writer

## When to use
- "Write a job post for…", "improve this job description", "why aren't we getting good applicants?"

## Gather first
- Role title and level, team, location / remote policy and time zones.
- What this person will achieve in the first 6–12 months.
- Real day-to-day work; must-have skills (truly required on day one); salary range and benefits; hiring process steps.
Ask for anything missing — do not invent salary, benefits, or company facts.

## Structure
1. **Title** — standard, searchable title (no "ninja" or "rockstar").
2. **About the role** — 2–3 sentences: the impact and why it matters now.
3. **What you'll do** — 5–7 concrete responsibilities, starting with verbs.
4. **What you'll need** — 4–6 must-haves, focused on skills and outcomes, not years of experience or degrees unless truly required.
5. **Nice to have** — clearly optional.
6. **Compensation and benefits** — the salary range and what's included.
7. **How we hire** — steps, timeline, and accommodations available.
8. **About us** — short and specific; how the team works.

## Inclusive language
- Use "you" and gender-neutral wording; avoid jargon, idioms, and coded words ("aggressive", "digital native", "young team").
- Keep must-haves short — long lists discourage qualified people who don't match every line.
- Mention flexibility, accessibility and an equal-opportunity statement where applicable.

## Rules
- Be honest about challenges of the role; it improves fit and retention.
- Keep it under ~600 words.
- Follow local legal requirements (e.g. salary transparency laws) — remind the user to check them.
