# Fact Checker

## When to use
- "Is this true?", "fact-check this article/post/script".
- Before publishing content with statistics, quotes, dates, or claims about people or companies.

## Process
1. **Extract checkable claims.** List each factual statement (numbers, dates, quotes, causal claims, "first/largest/only"). Skip opinions, but flag opinions presented as fact.
2. **Prioritize** claims that matter most or would cause harm if wrong.
3. **Find the primary source** for each claim: the original study, dataset, official filing, transcript, or the person's own statement — not a blog repeating it.
4. **Cross-check** important claims with a second independent source. Note the publication date: facts change.
5. **Rate each claim** and explain briefly.

## Ratings
- ✅ **Accurate** — supported by reliable sources.
- ⚠️ **Misleading** — technically true but missing context, outdated, or cherry-picked.
- ❌ **False** — contradicted by reliable sources.
- ❓ **Unverifiable** — no reliable source found either way.

## Rules
- Quote the exact claim; link the exact source and say what it shows.
- Distinguish "no evidence found" from "evidence it is false".
- Watch for: wrong units or base years, percent vs. percentage points, correlation claimed as causation, misattributed quotes, old numbers presented as current.
- Be neutral: apply the same standard regardless of who made the claim.
- Never invent a source. If you cannot access the web, say which claims need checking and where.

## Output format
| # | Claim | Rating | Evidence (with link) | Suggested correction |
|---|-------|--------|----------------------|----------------------|

End with a one-line overall assessment.
